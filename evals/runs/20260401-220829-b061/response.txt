Key risks to **OrderService** during the peak sale are mostly **downstream dependencies** that can slow, fail, or become inconsistent under load:

### 1) InventoryService (/reserve) is the highest risk dependency
OrderService must reserve stock before confirming an order.

**Why it’s risky right now**
- **Recent event-sourcing refactor**: stock is computed from an append-only event log instead of a mutable row. Under peak load, this often means heavier reads/aggregations and more writes per reservation.
- **Write amplification + fast-growing events table**: inventory-db-prod saw **~3× write amplification** after the refactor. During a sale, /reserve traffic spikes, which can accelerate event-log growth and increase I/O contention.
- **DB connection pressure**: inventory-db-prod pool max is **100**; InventoryService uses **60–70 connections at peak** already. A sale could push it toward saturation, causing queueing/timeouts that present to OrderService as slow /reserve or failures.

**How it breaks OrderService**
- Checkout latency increases (OrderService threads waiting on /reserve).
- Increased error rates (timeouts, 5xx) leading to failed checkouts.
- Potential for inconsistent order state if OrderService charges payment but cannot reserve (depends on your exact ordering/compensation flow).

### 2) inventory-db-prod capacity and performance risk (even after volume expansion)
**What we know**
- DB hit **92% storage utilization** recently and was expanded to **500GB**.
- Event log is append-only and growing quickly; partitioning is only “being evaluated.”

**Risks during peak**
- Rapid table growth can still cause performance degradation (bloat, slower scans, index growth, longer vacuums/checkpoints).
- If queries computing stock levels require scanning large event ranges, performance can cliff as the events table grows.

**How it breaks OrderService**
- InventoryService slows down or becomes unavailable → OrderService can’t complete orders.

### 3) PaymentGateway dependency under burst traffic
OrderService calls PaymentGateway during checkout.

**Risks**
- Payment processors often rate-limit or experience latency spikes during high-traffic events.
- Any increased retries from OrderService can amplify load (retry storms) and worsen the incident.

**How it breaks OrderService**
- Payment authorization failures/timeouts → checkout failures.
- If payment succeeds but inventory reservation fails (or vice versa), you can get customer-impacting edge cases unless compensations are robust.

### 4) Cross-system replenishment signals won’t help you in real time
Warehouse-system calls InventoryService /replenish, but during a flash sale:
- Replenishment timing is typically not aligned with demand spikes.
- If InventoryService is degraded, /replenish could also backlog/fail, worsening stock accuracy.

### Practical “downstream could break” checklist (most likely first)
1. **InventoryService /reserve latency/timeouts** due to DB connection saturation + event-log workload.
2. **inventory-db-prod I/O contention** and growth-related query slowdown.
3. **PaymentGateway latency/rate limits** causing retries and cascading timeouts.
